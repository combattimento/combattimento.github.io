# The Gross–Pitaevskii Equation

The **Gross–Pitaevskii equation (GPE)** describes the dynamics
of a dilute Bose–Einstein condensate at zero temperature.

For a condensate wavefunction $\psi(\mathbf{r},t)$, the
time-dependent equation is

$$
i\hbar \frac{\partial \psi(\mathbf{r},t)}{\partial t}
=
\left[
-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2
+ V(\mathbf{r})
+ g|\psi(\mathbf{r},t)|^2
\right]\psi(\mathbf{r},t).
$$

where

- $\psi(\mathbf{r},t)$ is the condensate order parameter.
- $\hbar$ is the reduced Planck constant.
- $m$ is the mass of a condensate particle.
- $V(\mathbf{r})$ is the external trapping potential.
- $g = \frac{4\pi\hbar^2 a_s}{m}$ is the contact interaction
  strength in three dimensions.
- $a_s$ is the s-wave scattering length.
- $\nabla^2$ is the Laplacian operator.

The nonlinear term $g|\psi|^2\psi$ accounts for interactions
between particles in the condensate.

## Physical Interpretation

The Gross–Pitaevskii equation is a nonlinear Schrödinger equation
that models the macroscopic quantum dynamics of a weakly
interacting Bose gas.

It is widely used to study condensate dynamics, collective
excitations, quantum vortices, and superfluidity.
